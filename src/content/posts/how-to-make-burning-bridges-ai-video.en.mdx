## What the Burning Bridges AI trend is

The source feel is a photorealistic, low-light party: a handheld camera moves through a packed room under a chandelier while the lead performer holds a metal cup, gestures, and interacts with people nearby. AI edits keep that camera path and room energy, then replace the lead — and optionally background models — with likenesses you have permission to use. Outfit-grid versions dress the lead in a fixed modern look while the rest of the crowd stays in urban everyday clothes.

Typical output format for social:

- About **29 seconds** (this template is ~**29 seconds**)
- **16:9** landscape in the source clip; crop or reframe in TikTok if you need **9:16**
- MP4, ready for TikTok / Reels / Shorts (mute the file and attach the trending sound in-app when needed)

## Before you upload: authorization comes first

Only use a **real-person photo when that person has authorized this use**.

- Prefer photos of **yourself**, or people who **explicitly allowed** you to generate and publish the video.
- Every person in every photo must be **18+**.
- Do **not** upload celebrities, public figures, or anyone who did not consent.
- Do **not** use the result to impersonate someone or spread misleading content.
- For multi-photo casts: each background face needs the same permission as the lead.

Genjutsu AI expects you to confirm rights over every likeness you upload. If you cannot show permission, pick another reference — pets and illustrated characters are common alternatives when a person photo is not appropriate.

## What you need

- **Burning Bridges template video** — the party-room handheld clip (or a licensed copy you may edit).
- **Lead photo** — clear shot of the person who replaces the central performer (face + build readable; the example workflow uses a man in a viking shirt and tan hat as the identity reference).
- **Background photos (optional but recommended)** — one distinct person per background role; do **not** reuse the same face twice.
- **Outfit reference (optional)** — a grid or stills for the lead wardrobe if you want a fixed fashion look.
- **Mode** — **Motion Transfer** (best when you keep the full camera path and restage multiple people).

Photo tips that improve the render:

- Lead: eyes visible, upper body in frame, sharp light, one subject
- Background: each file shows a different person — no duplicates
- JPG / PNG / WebP; avoid heavy filters and sunglasses on faces you need to keep

## Prompt and quality tips

**Do**

- Lock motion language: “one continuous handheld shot,” “same camera path,” “same timing”
- Map roles: lead = first photo; each background person = a separate photo
- Spell out the outfit list if you need a fashion-grid look
- Say “don’t duplicate” and “keep everyone individual” when using multiple faces

**Expect some AI limits**

- Crowd faces at the edge of frame are softer than the lead
- Watch straps, logos, and knit texture can simplify under low light
- Fast handheld motion is close, not frame-perfect

If a job fails or faces merge, retry with fewer background photos, sharper front-facing lead, and a shorter prompt that still names `@Video1` / `@Image1`. Check the on-screen credit estimate before each attempt.

Yes. Upload the party **video template**, add **one authorized lead photo**, choose **Motion Transfer**, and shorten the prompt to replace only the central performer. Skip the background-replacement lines if you want the original crowd to stay.
